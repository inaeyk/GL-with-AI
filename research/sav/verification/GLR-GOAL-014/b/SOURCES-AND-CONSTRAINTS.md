## Determinant and Fourier bookkeeping

Use dimensionless \(t,x,r,z\), \(f=1-1/r\), \(\lambda=4\), \(k=\pi/5\), orientation \(dt\wedge dx\wedge dz\), and \(u=t-x,\ v=t+x\). With \(\eta_b=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\), \(X_b=\eta_b H/f\) and \(d_b=H_{\theta\theta}/(2r^2)\), the EF radial reconstruction gives \(2H_{vr}+fH_{rr}=-2H_{\theta\theta}/r^2=-4d_b\). For \(s=\epsilon\cos kz\), write \(B=I+sX_b\) and \(R^2/r^2=1+2sd_b\) in the direct sector. In two noncompact dimensions, the antisymmetric two-inverse contraction is a determinant. Thus the density ratios for this sector are
\[
 M/r^2=\sqrt{\det B}(1+2sd_b)B^{-1}\eta_b,\qquad
 (PF)^{tx}/(P_0F)^{tx}=(1+2sd_b)^2/\sqrt{\det B}
\]
on a single noncompact input curl. Inserting \(\det B=1+s\operatorname{tr}X_b+s^2\det X_b\), with \(2\det X_b=(\operatorname{tr}X_b)^2-\operatorname{tr}X_b^2\), gives \(-X_b\eta_b,6d_b\) at first order. At second order the circle average gives
\[
 \tfrac12[X_b^2-(2d_b^2+\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}X_b^2)I]\eta_b,\qquad
 \tfrac12[14d_b^2+\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}X_b^2].
\]
A single \(+k\) insertion has half amplitude. The product of an unhalved \(\cos kz\) coefficient and \(A_{1,+}e^{ikz}+\overline{A_{1,+}}e^{-ikz}\) instead averages to \(\Re A_{1,+}\). The \(n=\pm2\) background coefficient times the baseline remains in \(n=\pm2\). The real pair carries twice the energy reported for one Fourier coefficient. By circle reflection \(A_z\) is odd in \(z\), so the returning mean compact component vanishes while the massive compact polarization is retained in the first insertion. The symbolic checks use an arbitrary \(\eta_b\)-self-adjoint \(2\times2\) endomorphism with the specified trace. [Source convention](https://github.com/inaeyk/GL-with-AI/blob/0b4bfcbb978b52626fb0816874df06055703c195/research/sav/GLR-GOAL-014/CURRENTS-AND-DATA.md) · [Check](check_sources.py).

The axial gauge addition \(h_a\mapsto h_a+R^2\partial_a\Lambda,\ h_T\mapsto h_T+R^2\Lambda\) cancels in \(A_a=h_a/R^2-\partial_a(h_T/R^2)\). The angular constraint follows from the divergence of the density equation by antisymmetry. For the mean radion, the block rescalings \(b_{\mu\nu}=e^{-2\phi/\sqrt3}b_{E,\mu\nu}\) and \(b_{zz}=e^{4\phi/\sqrt3}\) leave the three-dimensional orbit volume invariant. Each noncompact inverse metric cancels one \(R^2=e^{-2\phi/\sqrt3}r^2\) factor. These are identities in the ordered zero-mode sector. They do not remove the Einstein-frame mean mass and lapse or the quadratic nonzero-mode products.

## Mean constraint and the canonical upper jets

From the EF perturbation \(2(\mu/r-f\beta)dv^2+2\beta\,dv\,dr\), use \(dv=dt+dx,\ dr=f dx\). Its trace with respect to the background orbit metric is \(2\beta\). The inverse-density variation yields
\[
 M_{2,\mathrm{mean}}/r^2=(\beta-2\mu/(rf))
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad P_{2,\mathrm{mean}}^{tx}/P_0^{tx}=-\beta .
\]
Consequently \(\mathcal D^x_{\mathrm{mean}}=-\mathcal D^t_{\mathrm{mean}}\), \(G_{\mathrm{mean}}=0\) and
\[
 F_{\mathrm{mean}}=2(\beta-2\mu/(rf))(2w_{0,u}-fw_0/r),\qquad
 H_{\mathrm{mean}}=-fF_{\mathrm{mean}}/(2r)+\lambda f\beta w_0/r^2 .
\]
In the EF trace reversal \(T_{rr}=-Q_{rr}\) and \(T^v{}_v=fQ_{rr}/2+Q_{\theta\theta}/r^2+Q_{zz}/2\), the \(Q_{vr}\) term cancels. The factored mean integrands in the inherited implementation are \(\partial_r(e^{-2\Omega v}\mu)=-r^2e^{-2\Omega v}T^v{}_v/2\) and \(\partial_r(e^{-2\Omega v}\beta)=re^{-2\Omega v}T_{rr}/2\). Both are integrated from the asymptotic charge and clock conditions at fixed \(v\). The axial graph enters their evaluation time and radius but supplies no upper radial integration boundary. The \(r=2.5\) real outer mean boxes used by the source code include the recorded real contour enclosures. [Inherited mean check](https://github.com/inaeyk/GL-with-AI/blob/87fd711bf2e8117467caad1ff52c1cd344966974/research/sav/GLR-GOAL-011/checks/physical_overlap/mean_certificate.py) · [Outer enclosure](https://github.com/inaeyk/GL-with-AI/blob/87fd711bf2e8117467caad1ff52c1cd344966974/research/sav/GLR-GOAL-011/checks/exterior_source.txt).

In the fixed preparation \(v=r\), \(p=T'=-1/r\). Tangential vanishing at upper order gives \(U_x=-pU_t,\ A_{1z,x}=-pA_{1z,t}\) and \(A_{1x}=-pA_{1t}\). Zero compact electric density supplies
\((1-p^2)A_{1z,t}=ik(A_{1t}+pA_{1x})\).
Let \(d_k=\lambda+k^2r^2,\ D=r^2d_k\). Multiplying the time reconstruction by \(p\) and adding the space reconstruction cancels the compact term and yields \((1-p^2)U_t=pJ^t-J^x\). Subtracting \(p\) times the space equation from the time equation and using the compact density constraint replaces \(D\) by \(D-k^2r^4=\lambda r^2\) in the coefficient of \(A_{1t}+pA_{1x}\). This gives
\[
 A_{1z,t}=\frac{ik(J^t-pJ^x)}{\lambda r^2(1-p^2)},\qquad
 A_{1t}+pA_{1x}=\frac{J^t-pJ^x}{\lambda r^2}.
\]
The compact term enters even at zero displacement. In the two bulk polarizations the mixed \(t,x\) derivatives cancel by commutation. Differentiating \(L_c=r^4/D\) leaves \(ik\sqrt D\,\partial_x L_c/\sqrt W=2ik\sqrt\lambda f/d_k\), with \(W=\lambda r^4/d_k\). Its product with the exchange matrix is skew-Hermitian. The spatial operator is \((\partial_x+\mathsf A)^\dagger(\partial_x+\mathsf A)+K\). Setting \(k=0\) in the noncompact polarization gives exactly \(f(6/r^2-3/r^3)\), with no gyroscopic work term. [Canonical preparation](https://github.com/inaeyk/GL-with-AI/blob/87fd711bf2e8117467caad1ff52c1cd344966974/research/sav/GLR-GOAL-011/FINITE-DATA.md).

For the returning master the same tangential constraint fixes \((1-p^2)Q_{2z,t}=\mathcal D^x-p\mathcal D^t\). Under \((t,x)\mapsto(\tau,r)\) with \(\tau=t+x-r\), the Jacobian determinant is \(f\), so \(\mathcal D^\tau=(\mathcal D^t-p\mathcal D^x)/f,\ \mathcal D^r=\mathcal D^x\). The EF normal formula \(\mathcal D^r+(1/r)/(1+1/r)\mathcal D^\tau\) yields the same result. With \(w_2=2Q_{2z}/(\lambda r)\), one gets \(4w_{2,u}=G-b'F\), \(b'=-(1+p)/(1-p)\). Changing the denominator from \(1-p\) to \(1+p\) when expressing \(w_{2,u}\) in terms of \(\mathcal D^x-p\mathcal D^t\) would change the launch. Integrating on the graph with \(dx=dr/f\) and zero displacement gives the one-\(+k\) kinetic energy \(\int (1-p^2)(|U_t|^2/D+W|A_{1z,t}|^2)dr/(2f)\).

## Ray energies and weak exterior estimate

For a stationary scalar or mixed error variable, write \(D_x=\partial_x+\mathsf A\), energy density \(e=(|Y_t|^2+|D_xY|^2+K|Y|^2)/2\) and flux \(j=-\Re(Y_t^\dagger D_xY)\). On a fixed-\(u\) ray its density is \((e-j)/2=|Y_v+\mathsf A Y/2|^2+K|Y|^2/4\) in \(dv\). The factor \(1/2\) comes from \(dx=dv/2\). The same identity applies to the stationary time commutations. The physical lower flux roots inherited from GOAL-013 are \(10^{13}\) for scalar orders \(0,1,2\) and \(10^{48}\) for mixed orders \(0,1\). Since \(V\ge2/r^2\), the scalar ray combination \(B_0=(|w_0|+|w_{0,t}|+|w_{0,tt}|)/r+|w_{0,v}|+|w_{0,tv}|\) costs \((3\sqrt2+2)10^{13}<8\cdot10^{13}\). For \(r\ge100\), \(K\ge1/4\) gives \(\|Y\|_2,\|Y_t\|_2\le4\cdot10^{48}\). Removing \(\mathsf A/2\) from the two differentiated fluxes costs at most \(4\cdot10^{48}/100\) each, so \(B_m=|Y|+|Y_t|+|Y_v|+|Y_{tv}|\) costs less than \(12\cdot10^{48}\). The argument holds for finite segments of every ray and the infinite segments follow by monotone integration. [Inherited coefficient and flux estimates](https://github.com/inaeyk/GL-with-AI/blob/1e8e628f68af4cab174fcc44b81af4748a22115c/research/sav/GLR-GOAL-013/investigators/B-SOURCE.md).

To check the reconstruction bounds, use \(w_x=2w_v-w_t\), \(w_{tx}=2w_{tv}-w_{tt}\) and \(w_{xx}=w_{tt}+Vw\). These bound \(rA_0\) and each of its first \(t,x,v\) derivatives by \(10rB_0\). The first-insertion mass entries are bounded by \(2h\), their derivatives by \(3h\). Thus \(rJ/D=2\widetilde M_1(rA_0)/d_k\) and its \(v\) derivative fit the bound \(100hrB_0\). In the mixed forcing the two differentiated mass products in each row have radial multipliers bounded by one after multiplying by \(2/\sqrt{d_k}\) or \(2krL_c/\sqrt W\). Their log radial factors are bounded by \(5/r\). Each product uses two matrix entries and the curl source uses \(|X|\le3h|w_0|/r\). This leaves slack below the \(10^4hrB_0\) bound on the two-row source. For \(\partial_v(rA_1)\), use
\[
 Y_{xv}=Y_{tv}+\tfrac12(V_{\rm eff}Y-C\sigma_1Y_t+S_1).
\]
The derivative multipliers of \(rA_1\) are bounded by \(1/2\), and their first \(v\) derivatives by \(1/r\). With the \(\mathsf A\) bounds these give \(30B_m+10^5hrB_0\) for the sum of the value and derivative bounds. For \(F\) and \(G\), there are two potential rows and two entries per row, with a common factor of two. Using the \(100(h^2+b_*)\) value and derivative coefficient caps gives a bound on \(F_v,G_v\) by \(1200hB_m+4.016\cdot10^6(h^2+b_*)rB_0\). The undifferentiated currents cost less. In \(H\) the mass term has an extra \(f/r\) and one row. The curl term is bounded with \(2f/r^3\), \(P_1/P_0=6d_b\), \(|U|\le 3r^2|Y|\), and \(|Q_{0z}|=2r|w_0|\). These terms stay within the quoted common \(10^4hB_m+10^8(h^2+b_*)rB_0\). This construction uses fixed-\(u\) control of \(Y_t\) to obtain \(Y_x\). The opposite null derivative flux alone would give a different norm.

For \(\ell=v-c\ge0\), the causal geometry gives \(r\ge r_c+0.49\ell\) and \(r\le r_c+5+\ell/2\). The inherited coefficient envelopes give the rates \(e^{-\ell/6}\) and \(e^{-\ell/3}\). Absorb the final factor \(r\) in the quadratic and mean term with \(r\le(r_c+5)e^{\ell/12}\). Calling the resulting mixed and scalar \(L^2_v\) amplitudes \(D_m,D_0\), the exact Cauchy–Schwarz weights are \(\sqrt3\) for \(e^{-\ell/6}\) and \(\sqrt2\) for \(e^{-\ell/4}\). Thus the \(F,H\) ray integral bound is \(\sqrt3D_m+\sqrt2D_0\), including \(u=d\). Since \(F,G\) and their \(v\) derivatives are \(L^2\) on each half-line, their continuous \(H^1\) representatives tend to zero at infinity. Integrating \(\partial_v|q|^2\) back from infinity gives \(\sup |q|\le\sqrt2(D_m+D_0)\) for \(q=F,G\). Uniformity over the finite \(u\) interval also gives \(G_\infty=0\) in \(L^2_u\). Independent arithmetic confirms \(3.354\times10^{-15}\) and \(2.739\times10^{-15}\) at \(400r_0\). At \(100r_0\) the same absolute estimates are too broad to use. ## Adverse control and gate

For a smooth scalar stream function \(\Phi\) vanishing near the graph, take \(F=\Phi_v,\ G=-\Phi_u,\ H=0\). Then the strong source vanishes identically and \(w_2=0\) solves the zero-data problem for the physical scalar potential as well as for \(V=0\). Choosing \(\Phi=Bue^{1-v}\) for \(v\ge c=1\) with a smooth continuation below the cap produces \(\int_1^\infty F(1,v)\,dv=-B\) on \(u\in[0,1]\). The strong source norm can vanish at any amplitude of the terminal integral. In the free \(g=u\) test, four times the exterior pairing splits into the terminal \(-B\), bulk \(B/2\) and cap \(B/2\). Its sum vanishes. This illustrates why an exterior weak bound must control the separate currents and why discarding an interface term in a strong-source split changes the calculation. The control is a statement about the information in the scalar source norm. Its current is manufactured. [Exact check](check_sources.py).

The separate exterior estimate survives as a conditional fixed-cut theorem and directed arithmetic. It complements the independently audited GOAL-013 strong exterior bound by controlling individual currents and a terminal ray. Neither is a compact accuracy estimate. For the earliest physical sign, the next missing B-side input is a conforming compact lower reconstruction with outward mean and GL coefficient errors, both induced mixed graph jets and a bound on the coupled residual, including its terminal-ray trace. For the weak target one can contract the coefficient and lower-field errors with the volume adjoint and terminal row before bounding them. For the strong target one must also propagate the relevant commuted and seam errors into the returning cap trace. The conserved charges and the fixed frame are hypotheses throughout. The ordered calculation gives no nonlinear constraint-family existence or uniform solution-to-news differentiability.
