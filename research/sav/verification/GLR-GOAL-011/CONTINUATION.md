## Physical half-plane and Cauchy pairing

Set $r_0=1$, $f=1-1/r$, $x=r+\log(r-1)$, $u=t-x$, $v=t+x$, and use $e^{-st}$ for the forward transform. The axial data have compact support on $t=T(x)=r-x$. This includes the induced jets because Gauss completion is local. Take $u_-,v_-$ below the minima on the launch support, so finite propagation through the triangular equations confines every axial field and local source to $u\ge u_-$, $v\ge v_-$. A constant $t$ above the latest launch intersects a smooth zero extension to the past of the graph. At a nonlaunch graph point any possible incoming axial signal lies strictly to the future by spacelikeness. At finite $t$ the interval of support is contained in $[v_--t,t-u_-]$.

After scalar projection at orders zero and two and normalization of the massive pair at order one, the unknown fields have wave principal part and bounded stationary lower-order coefficients with bounded derivatives. On the support any finite power $f^{-j}$ from source and reconstruction is bounded by $C_j e^{jt}$, and $r$ by $C(1+t)$. The pinned EF coefficients and their finite derivatives then give at most exponential growth. For each inhomogeneous equation commute a finite number of derivatives and use $E'(t)\le C E(t)+C\|S(t)\|_{H^m}^2$, with $\|w\|_{H^m}^2$ included in $E$. By the order of the triangular system one may choose enough lower-order derivatives before solving each energy estimate. Sobolev embedding gives the requisite finite jets on a fixed line $x=X$ with exponential $u$-bounds. At the left, causality gives $|\widehat S(s,x)|\le C_s e^{(\Re s-c)x}$ for $x\ll0$, by integrating from $v_--x$ and taking $\Re s>c$.

At the right, use $w_{uv}=A w_u+B w_v+D w+S$ for the massive pair, with $A,B\to0$, bounded $D$, and $u_-$ a zero-data line in $x\ge X$. In the weighted supremum norm $e^{-\gamma(u-u_-)-\alpha(x-X)}$, the characteristic integration lengths are bounded by $K=1/(\gamma-\alpha/2)$ in $u$ and $L=2/\alpha$ in $v$. The factors of $1/2$ follow from $x=(v-u)/2$. If $G$ bounds the weighted right-hand side and $U,V,W$ are the norms of $w_u,w_v,w$, the integral equations give $U\le U_b+LG$, $V\le KG$, $W\le W_b+LKG$. Choose $X$ and then $\gamma$ so $\|A\|L+K(\|B\|+\|D\|L)<1$. These inequalities hold on each bounded region $u\ge u_-$, $u\le U_1$, $v\ge u+2X$, $v\le V_1$. All the integration segments stay in that region. This proves bounds uniform in $V_1$. For the scalar stages take $\alpha=0$ and use $W\le U/\gamma$ from the $u$-integration. In the equation for $U$ the potential has a uniformly finite $v$-integral, so its contribution can be absorbed at large $\gamma$.

Write $q=\sqrt{k^2+\Omega^2}$ for the GL exterior exponent. At fixed $u$ the growing GL field has size $e^{\Omega u-(q-\Omega)x}$ up to powers of $r$. For the massive stage choose $0<\alpha<q-\Omega$ with slack. Multiplying its allowed $e^{\alpha x}$ growth by the second GL factor makes the returning source exponentially integrable in $x$. The direct quadratic and mean sources have stronger decay. Repeating the estimates for finitely many derivatives yields a bound $C e^{\gamma(u-u_-)-b x}$, $b>0$, for the right-hand strong source. In the massless equation this proves convergence of $w_u$ as $v\to\infty$ by integration in $v$, and convergence of $w$ by integration in $u$. Also $w_v\to0$ on each finite cut from integration of the decaying right-hand side in $u$. All three fields are dominated by $C e^{\gamma(u-u_-)}$ uniformly on $x\ge X$. This gives the same null trace for $w_t=w_u+w_v$ as for $w_u$, and dominated convergence for the unwindowed physical transform
$$
 {\cal T}(s)=\int_{u_-}^{\infty}e^{-su}w_{\mathscr I}(u)\,du
           =\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{sx}\widehat{\Theta w}(s,x),
 \qquad \Re s>\gamma.
$$
The exponential growth assumption concerns ordered jets. In the physical right half-plane the left source estimate above is integrable against $h\sim e^{sx}$, and at the right $\widehat S=O(e^{-(\Re s+b)x})$. The Wronskian convention $W=hg'-h'g$ yields derivative jump $+1$ for $h(x_<)g(x_>)/W$. Its right-hand Green contribution from $y>x$ tends to zero after multiplication by $e^{sx}$, since its absolute bound is $O(e^{2\Re s\,x})\int_x^\infty e^{-(2\Re s+b)y}\,dy$. Thus the outgoing transform really is the normalized source and Cauchy pairing divided by $W$.

A sign check independent of radial quadrature follows by testing the graph distribution with $e^{-s t}y(x)$. The graph contribution is

## The two shifted pencils and the outer contour

## Scope of the conclusion and a falsification control

At a simple zero with holomorphic $I$ the coefficient is $I(s_q)/W'(s_q)$ in the stated shear and time-origin convention. It is the residue of the analytically continued physical unwindowed transform for the fixed preparation, if the claimed signed source enclosure is validated. News multiplies it by $s_q$; reality supplies the conjugate. Finite-cut tapering determines a different continuation in general. Subtracting the two Laurent terms with a chosen reference cut leaves a transform analytic at this pole pair. A separate estimate is required to control that remainder on a time window. The ordered transform and its poles make no uniform claim about a finite GL seed after prolonged growth.

An explicit control separates a nonzero finite-cut waveform from a nonzero pole. In the stationary scalar test problem, take a real smooth compact forcing above the launch graph that gives a nonzero outgoing trace, and apply $(\partial_t-s_q)(\partial_t-\overline{s_q})$ to that forcing. Its causal solution is the same operator applied to the original solution. There are no time endpoint terms since the source is compact and the response vanishes in the past. The new numerator is multiplied by $(s-s_q)(s-\overline{s_q})$, so the fundamental pair has zero residue. The new trace still cannot vanish identically: otherwise the original smooth causal trace would satisfy a homogeneous second-order ODE and have zero Cauchy data on an earlier cut. Such an auxiliary forcing is a control on logical inference, outside the fixed vacuum hierarchy.
