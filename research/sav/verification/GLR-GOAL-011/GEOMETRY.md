## Warped-product axial equation

On a round background $b_{ab}dy^a dy^b+R^2\gamma_{AB}d\theta^A d\theta^B$, take the axial metric coefficients $h_aX_A$ and $2h_TX_{AB}$. The gauge change generated by $\Lambda(y)X^A$ adds $R^2\partial_a\Lambda$ to $h_a$ and $R^2\Lambda$ to $h_T$. Thus $A_a=h_a/R^2-\partial_a(h_T/R^2)$ is invariant under this angular diffeomorphism. Put $F=dA$. The unit sphere identities are
$$
 D^A X_A=0,\qquad D^A X_{AB}=-\frac{\lambda}{2}X_B,\qquad
 \int |X|^2=\ell(\ell+1),\qquad
 \int |X_{AB}|^2=\frac{\lambda}{2}\int |X|^2 ,
 \quad \lambda=\ell(\ell+1)-2 .
$$
The divergence identity follows by commuting the sphere derivatives, and the final identity follows by integrating $X^B D^A X_{AB}$ by parts.

One way to reconstruct the coefficients is to set $h_T=0$ and write the angular part in the shifted coframe $d\theta^A+A_a X^A dy^a$. With $h=R^2\gamma$, the horizontal brackets are $[e_a,e_b]=-F_{ab}X$, and the fiber deformation tensor is
$$
 B_{aAB}=\tfrac12(\partial_a h-\mathcal L_{A_a X}h)_{AB}
        =RR_a\gamma_{AB}-R^2 A_a X_{AB}.
$$
The horizontal bracket contributes $-\tfrac14 h_{AB}X^AX^B F_{ab}F^{ab}$ to the scalar curvature. For example the vertical part of $\nabla_{e_a}e_b$ is $-F_{ab}X/2$ by the Koszul formula. The horizontal part of $\nabla_A\partial_B$ is $-B^a{}_{AB}e_a$. Contracting the curvature and integrating the horizontal divergence against $\sqrt{|b|}\sqrt{\det h}$ leaves the deformation terms $-\operatorname{tr}(B_a B^a)+\operatorname{tr}(B_a)\operatorname{tr}(B^a)$, with sphere indices raised by $h^{-1}$. The terms linear in $A_a$ in these traces vanish by $D_AX^A=0$. The quadratic orbit metric change implicit in the shifted coframe multiplies the background Einstein equation in the second variation, which vanishes through the retained spherical vacuum orders. Hence the quadratic axial action, after factoring out $\int |X|^2$, is
$$
 \int \sqrt{|b|}\left[-\frac14 R^4 F_{ab}F^{ab}
                 -\frac{\lambda}{2}R^2 A_a A^a\right]d^3y .
$$
This agrees with the general warped vector equations with $n=2$, $F_a^{\rm IK}=RA_a$, and $k_V^2=\ell(\ell+1)-1$. The $\ell=1$ Killing limit removes the deformation term and gives Maxwell. The dual scalar normalization used below requires $\ell\ge2$. [Ishibashi–Kodama, §5.1, (5.2)–(5.3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.6148v2)

Varying the action gives
$$
 E^a=\partial_b\mathcal P^{ba}-\lambda M^{ab}A_b=0,\quad
 \mathcal P^{ba}=\sqrt{|b|}R^4 F^{ba},\quad
 M^{ab}=\sqrt{|b|}R^2 b^{ab}.
$$
Its divergence enforces $\partial_a(M^{ab}A_b)=0$ by antisymmetry. Equivalently the angular equation comes from the diffeomorphism identity before setting $h_T=0$. Expanding these exact densities yields $E_0A_1=-E_1A_0$ and $E_0\langle A_2\rangle=-\langle E_1A_1+E_2A_0\rangle$. The inverse metric expansion is $b^{-1}=G_0-\epsilon G_0HG_0+\epsilon^2(G_0HG_0HG_0-G_0B G_0)$. The corresponding density expansion follows from $\exp[\frac12\operatorname{tr}\log(I+\epsilon G_0 H+\epsilon^2G_0B)]$. This gives the linear $B$ and direct $H^2$ terms of the pinned hierarchy. The real cosine has Fourier coefficients $1/2$ at each of $\pm k$, so the mean of two full cosine amplitudes has the factor $1/2$. The quadratic spherical $n=\pm2$ background deformation times the $n=0$ baseline has no mean. Reflection makes the mean compact axial slot vanish and preserves the mixed sine compact slot.

With the stated Lorentzian Hodge convention $*^2=-1$ on orbit one- and two-forms. For $Q=R^4*F$ the equation is $dQ=-\lambda R^2*A$, equivalently $A=R^{-2}*dQ/\lambda$. Applying $F=dA$ gives
$$
 D_b\!\left[R^{-2}(dQ)^{ba}\right]=\lambda R^{-4}Q^a .
$$
Taking its divergence yields the dual constraint. These signs give $Q_z=-r^4F_{tr}$ on the product background, with $t$ replaced by $v=t+x$ giving the same $F_{vr}$.

## Mean Einstein-frame geometry and source coefficients

Write the Ricci expansion as $R^{(5)}_{MN}|_{\epsilon^2}=R^{(5)}_{\rm lin}[B]_{MN}+Q_{MN}(H,H)$, where here $Q_{MN}(H,H)$ denotes the quadratic Ricci coefficient, distinct from the dual axial one-form. At $n=0$ the compact odd shift has no mean. Put $c=\langle B_{zz}\rangle$ and $m_{\mu\nu}=\langle B_{\mu\nu}\rangle+\frac12\bar g_{\mu\nu}c$. In the product background the five-dimensional linearized Ricci components are
$$
 R^{(5)}_{\mu\nu,\rm lin} = R^{(4)}_{\mu\nu,\rm lin}[\langle B\rangle]
                       -\tfrac12\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu c,\qquad
 R^{(5)}_{zz,\rm lin}=-\tfrac12\Box c .
$$
The four-dimensional conformal Ricci variation associated with $c\bar g/2$ adds $-\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu c/2-\bar g_{\mu\nu}\Box c/4$. Thus $\Box c=2\langle Q_{zz}\rangle$ and $R^{(4)}_{\rm lin}[m]_{\mu\nu}=-\langle Q_{\mu\nu}\rangle-\frac12\bar g_{\mu\nu}\langle Q_{zz}\rangle$. Trace reversal gives
$$
 \mathcal T_{\mu\nu}=-\langle Q_{\mu\nu}\rangle+
          \tfrac12\bar g_{\mu\nu}\langle \operatorname{tr}_5 Q\rangle .
$$
Conservation uses the linear vacuum equation for $H$, as required by the second-order Bianchi identity.

For $ds_E^2=-e^{2\beta}(1-2m_{\rm MS}/r)dv^2+2e^\beta dv\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2$, the warped Einstein tensor gives $G_{rr}=2\beta_r/r$, $G^v{}_v=-2m_{{\rm MS},r}/r^2$, $G^r{}_v=2m_{{\rm MS},v}/r^2$. The first two identities also follow from the spherical two-dimensional tensor
$$
 G_{ij}=-\frac{2}{r}\nabla_i\nabla_j r+
     \left(\frac{2}{r}\Box r-\frac{1-(\nabla r)^2}{r^2}\right)g_{ij}.
$$
With $m_{\rm MS}=r_0/2+\epsilon^2\mu$, the fixed mass and normalized clock set the homogeneous integration constants to zero. Hence $\mu=\tfrac12\int_r^\infty R^2\mathcal T^v{}_v\,dR$ and $\beta=-\tfrac12\int_r^\infty R\mathcal T_{RR}\,dR$ at fixed $v$, with $\mu_v=r^2\mathcal T^r{}_v/2$ from conservation and the vanishing endpoint flux. Substitution of the inverse EF metric into the trace gives $\mathcal T^v{}_v=f Q_{rr}/2+Q_{\theta\theta}/r^2+Q_{zz}/2$ and $\mathcal T_{rr}=-Q_{rr}$, with all $Q$ averaged.

The compact terms in the quadratic mean can be checked using the extrinsic curvature $K_{\mu\nu}=\partial_z g_{\mu\nu}/2$ of the $z$ leaves in the unitary GL gauge. Their contribution to $R^{(5)}_{\mu\nu}$ is $-\partial_z K_{\mu\nu}-K K_{\mu\nu}+2K_{\mu\rho}K^\rho{}_\nu$, and to $R^{(5)}_{zz}$ it is $-\partial_z K-\operatorname{tr}(K^2)$. The total derivatives integrate to zero. Since $K_{\mu\nu}=-\epsilon k H_{\mu\nu}\sin(kz)/2$ at first order, the surviving coefficients are $k^2(H^2)_{\mu\nu}/4-k^2 H_{\mu\nu}\operatorname{tr}_4(H)/8$ and $-k^2\operatorname{tr}_4(H^2)/8$. The noncompact warped formulas for $W=R^2$ are $R^{(4)}_{ij}=R^{(2)}_{ij}-W^{-1}\nabla_i\nabla_jW+W_iW_j/(2W^2)$ and $R^{(4)}_{\theta\theta}=1-\frac12\Box_2W$. These formulae provide an analytic check of the decomposition in the pinned mean solver.

A direct 5-by-5 Christoffel/Ricci calculation provides a separate implementation check. The manufactured profile is $(H_{vv},H_{vr},H_{rr},H_{\theta\theta})=(r^2-r/2,(r-1)^2,r^3/11,2r-r^2/5)e^{13v/137}\cos(11z/19)$ at $r_0=1$. Compute the $\epsilon^2$ coefficients from the full five-dimensional inverse, first and second coordinate derivatives, and the Ricci contraction. Average at compact phases $0,\pi/2$. For these quadratic coefficients the average is exact because the only nonzero Fourier types are the constant and second cosine at the evaluated diagonal slots. At $r=1.020,1.612,2.500,7.230$ the maximum discrepancy in $(Q_{rr},Q_{\theta\theta},Q_{zz},\mathcal T^v{}_v,\mathcal T_{rr})$, scaled by $\max(1,|\text{direct}|)$ componentwise, was $8.882\times10^{-16}$. The manufactured jet checks the algebra. It need not satisfy the linear vacuum equations and tests neither the physical GL input nor its interval bounds.

For the pure mean radion put $\alpha=\phi_2/\sqrt3$ at the retained order, with $\phi_2=\sqrt3 c/4$. In the orbit, $b_{ij}=e^{-2\alpha}(b_E)_{ij}$ on the two noncompact slots, $b_{zz}=e^{4\alpha}$, and $R=e^{-\alpha}r$. Then $\sqrt{|b|}=\sqrt{|b_E|}$. The factors $R^2 b^{ij}$ and $R^4 b^{ik}b^{jl}$ are individually invariant under this change. The same cancellation applies to the axial exact curl $Q_z$. The radion stress in the four-dimensional Einstein equation starts at $\epsilon^4$. These statements isolate the linear mean radion term and leave the quadratic nonzero-mode GL products in the source.

For those products, let $g=b_0^{-1}$ on the EF two-orbit, $X=gH$, $S=H_{\theta\theta}/r^2$, and $t_X=\operatorname{tr}X=-2S$ by tracelessness of the unitary GL tangent. The determinant expansion is
$$
 \sqrt{\det(1+\epsilon X)}
 =1+\epsilon t_X/2+\epsilon^2(t_X^2/8-\operatorname{tr}(X^2)/4).
$$
On a two-form the noncompact curl density is exactly $-R^4F_{vr}/\sqrt{|b_{2d}|}$ when $b_{zz}=1$. Insert $R^2/r^2=1+\epsilon S$ for the direct part and apply the inverse determinant factor. The first-order multiplier is $3S$ and the quadratic one is $7S^2/2+\operatorname{tr}(X^2)/4$. In the mass density the scalar first variation is $S+t_X/2=0$. The first-order endomorphism is $-X$ and the second-order one is $X^2-(S^2/2+\operatorname{tr}(X^2)/4)I$. With $\langle\cos^2(kz)\rangle=1/2$ these give the factors and signs in the pinned $HJ$ and $H^2P$ source table. In the Einstein mean term, $\sqrt{|b_{2d}|}=1+\epsilon^2\beta$ with no change of $R^2=r^2$ in the EF area gauge. This gives $-\beta$ for the curl multiplier and $\beta I-gB_E$ for the mass endomorphism. This mean already incorporates circle averaging.

## Canonical data and return equation

In coordinates adapted to the spacelike slice, write the future normal as $n=N^{-1}(\partial_\tau-N^i\partial_i)$. Since $\sqrt{|b|}=N\sqrt{\gamma}$ and $n_a=-N(d\tau)_a$, the density in the problem is $\pi^i=\mathcal P^{\tau i}$. The normal equation becomes
$$
 A_n=\frac{\partial_i\pi^i}{\lambda\sqrt\gamma R^2},
 \qquad A_\tau=N A_n+N^i A_i.
$$
Given the tangential $A_i$, the prescribed $\pi^i$, and $F_{ij}=(d_\Sigma A)_{ij}$, the electric equation gives $F_{\tau i}=N^j F_{ji}-N\gamma_{ij}\pi^j/(\sqrt\gamma R^4)$. It fixes $\partial_\tau A_i$ after adding $\partial_i A_\tau$. The weighted divergence has nonzero coefficient $M^{\tau\tau}$ and fixes the normal derivative of $A_\tau$. This shows what is freely prescribed and what is induced. The corresponding dual densities are $Q_z=\pi^r$, $Q_r=-\pi^z$, $\widetilde\pi^z=-\lambda A_r$, and $\widetilde\pi^r=\lambda A_z$ in these oriented coordinates.

For $\tau=v-r=0$, use $s_r=r_0/r$. The product inverse orbit metric has $(b^{\tau\tau},b^{\tau r},b^{rr})=(-(1+s_r),s_r,f)$ and density one. Write $Q_z=\lambda r\Psi/2$. The $a=\tau,r$ equations with zero source give
$$
 \lambda r^2[-(1+s_r)A_\tau+s_r A_r]=-\partial_rQ_z,\qquad
 \lambda r^2[s_rA_\tau+f A_r]=\partial_\tau Q_z.
$$
Eliminating $A_\tau$ gives the pinned tangential $A_r=[(1+s_r)rW-s_r(ra)_{,r}]/(2r^2)$ for $\Psi|_\Sigma=a$, $\partial_\tau\Psi|_\Sigma=W$. The characteristic identity on the graph is $(\Psi_t,\Psi_x)=(W,-W)$ since $u_\Sigma=T(x)-x$ and $T'=-s_r$. The canonical choices give no independent mixed or returning pairs. The constrained normal fields still depend on $\epsilon$, and solving for the full derivatives is necessary.

In $(t,r)$ let $\mathcal C=(\langle P_1F_1+P_2F_0\rangle)^{tr}$ and $\mathcal D^a=\lambda\langle M_1^{ab}A_{1b}+M_2^{ab}A_{0b}\rangle$. The *exact* mean $Q_{2z}=-r^4\langle F_{2,tr}\rangle+\mathcal C$ makes the two return equations
$$
 \lambda r^2 A_{2t}=f(\partial_r Q_{2z}+\mathcal D^t),\qquad
 \lambda r^2 f A_{2r}=\partial_t Q_{2z}-\mathcal D^r .
$$
Substituting into $F_{2,tr}=\partial_t A_{2r}-\partial_r A_{2t}$ yields
$$
 \left(-\partial_t^2+\partial_x^2-f(6/r^2-3r_0/r^3)\right)\frac{2 Q_{2z}}{\lambda r}
 =\frac{2r}{\lambda}\left[
   -\partial_t(r^{-2}\mathcal D^r)-\partial_x(fr^{-2}\mathcal D^t)
   -\lambda f r^{-4}\mathcal C\right].
$$
This follows by applying $\partial_x=f\partial_r$ and using $Q_{2z}=\lambda r\Psi_2/2$, which cancels the first $x$ derivative. On $\tau=0$, the returning data have $Q_{2z}=A_{2r}^{(\tau,r)}=0$. The $\tau$ component gives $A_{2\tau}=\mathcal D^\tau/[\lambda r^2(1+s_r)]$, so $\partial_\tau Q_{2z}=\mathcal D^r+s_r\mathcal D^\tau/(1+s_r)$. In $(t,x)$ the tangential condition is $A_{2x}+T'A_{2t}=0$, giving the unnormalized normal contact $(\partial_t+T'\partial_x)Q_{2z}=\mathcal D^r-T'f\mathcal D^t$. For $p=\mathcal D^r/r^2,\ q=f\mathcal D^t/r^2$ and $\varphi=rw$, the volume integration of $-\varphi(p_t+q_x)$ over $t\ge T(x)$ creates $+\varphi(p-T'q)$ on the graph. It cancels the opposite Green datum, including its sign and factor $2/\lambda$.

The same check works before the return projection. For a zero upper tangential potential on the graph, $d(\Theta A_j)=\Theta F_j$ since $d\Theta\wedge A_j=0$. The step derivative of the full electric flux vanishes at upper orders because its exact density coefficient is zero on the graph. The lower density and upper background flux terms must therefore be combined before dropping the step term. For the baseline scalar let $a(x)=a_\delta(T(x)-x)$. From $\partial_t\Theta=\delta(t-T)$ and $\partial_x\Theta=-T'\delta(t-T)$ one obtains the radial source
$$
 e^{-\sigma T}\big[(1-T')a_x-(\sigma(1-T'^2)+T'')a\big]
$$
after transforming the zero extension. In the background reconstruction the physical first derivatives are $\widehat{\Theta\Psi_t}=\sigma\widehat\Psi-e^{-\sigma T}a$ and $\widehat{\Theta\Psi_x}=\partial_x\widehat\Psi+T'e^{-\sigma T}a$. Differentiation of the split Green expression contributes $(1-T')e^{-\sigma T}a$ before adding the last $T'e^{-\sigma T}a$. Thus the net spatial contact in the pinned split formula is $+e^{-\sigma T}a$.

## Two-polarization intermediate

In $(t,x,z)$ the background orbit is $f(-dt^2+dx^2)+dz^2$. Use the full real cosine amplitude in the $+k$ equations by writing the mixed quantities with $\partial_z=ik$. Call the mixed transform argument $s_1$ and use primes for $d/dx$. The first insertion contributes the stationary density correction $X^{tx}$ and the two noncompact mass-source entries $y^t,y^x$. Its $tz,xz$ and mass $z$ entries vanish because $H_{zM}=0$ and $A_{0z}=0$. The total mixed density has $\mathcal P^{tx}=-\mathcal U$, where $\mathcal U=r^4F_{1,tx}/f-X^{tx}$. The other fluxes are $\mathcal P^{tz}=-r^4(s_1A_z-ik A_t)$ and $\mathcal P^{xz}=r^4(A_z'-ik A_x)$. With $d=\lambda+k^2 r^2,\ D=r^2d$ the $t,x$ equations solve as
$$
 A_t=\frac{y^t-\mathcal U'-ik r^4s_1 A_z}{D},\qquad
 A_x=\frac{-y^x-s_1\mathcal U-ik r^4 A_z'}{D}.
$$
The remaining $z$ equation is $s_1\mathcal P^{tz}+(\mathcal P^{xz})'-\lambda fr^2 A_z=0$. Its consistency with the first two gives the sourced Gauss law by taking their divergence. To normalize the two dynamic fields set
$$
 L_c=\frac{r^4}{D},\quad W_c=\frac{\lambda r^4}{d},\quad
 (Y,Z)=\big(\mathcal U/\sqrt D,\sqrt{W_c}A_z\big),\quad
 a=(\log\sqrt D)',\ b=(\log\sqrt{W_c})',\ c=\frac{ik L_c}{\sqrt{W_c}}.
$$
The curl definition gives the normalized $Y$ potential $V_Y=f(k^2+\lambda/r^2)+a^2-a'$ and coupling $-s_1(2ik\sqrt\lambda f/d)Z$ in $Y''$. The $z$ equation gives $V_Z=f(k^2+\lambda/r^2)+b^2+b'$ and the same off-diagonal multiplier of $Y$. For instance the unnormalized cross terms on the left of the curl and $z$ equations are $ik s_1 D L_c' A_z$ and $ik s_1 L_c' \mathcal U$. Using $J_Y=Y'-y^t/\sqrt D$ and $J_Z=Z'+c y^x$, the first-order source in the position equation is $F=(y^t/\sqrt D,-c y^x)$ and in the momentum equation it is
$$
 G=\left(\frac{s_1 y^x-a y^t}{\sqrt D}+\frac{f\sqrt D}{r^4}X^{tx},
             -c(s_1y^t+b y^x)\right).
$$
This reproduces the combination $G+s_1F$ used for regular horizon transport. The $k=0$ curl channel reduces to RW and the mixed couplings vanish. For $k\ne0$ the off-diagonal multiplier forces $Z$ for a generic $Y$. Zero freely prescribed compact canonical data alone cannot justify discarding it. In the physical right Laplace half-plane there is a positive conserved energy from the static Proca action. Its density in a static slice is $N\sqrt\gamma[R^4(\tfrac12|F_{ni}|^2+\tfrac14|F_{ij}|^2)+\tfrac12\lambda R^2(|A_n|^2+|A_i|^2)]$. A decaying eigenfunction at both ends with $\Re s_1>0$ would grow as $e^{2\Re s_1t}$ in that energy with zero endpoint flux, so it vanishes. This argument is restricted to the physical decaying branches and does not certify shifted noncoincidence on the continued sheet.

## Extraction, qualifications, and reproduction

On fixed outgoing cuts in the common Einstein-frame Bondi coordinates, write $h_r^{(u,r)}=0$ in the axial angular gauge, $2h_T=r C(u)+o(r)$ and $h_T/r^2\to0$. The transformed product reconstruction gives $A_r^{(u,r)}=(\partial_r Q_z)_u/(\lambda r^2)$, whereas angular completion gives $A_r^{(u,r)}=-\partial_r(h_T/r^2)$. Integrating by parts from infinity with $Q_z/r\to\lambda\Psi_{\mathscr I}/2$ yields $h_T/r\to\Psi_{\mathscr I}/2$. Hence $C_{AB}=\Psi_{\mathscr I}X_{AB}$ and the news is its physical retarded-time derivative. At the ordered returning level the extraction uses decay of the lower products on the same cuts, as required in the trace audit. The zero-mode mean radion cancels as above. The unit real $\ell=2$ tensor norm is $12$, giving the physical tensor flux $12\dot C^2/(32\pi G_4)$. The raw scalar $r^3F_{tr}$ has the conversion $-2/\lambda$ and would overstate flux by four if treated as the shear. [Martel–Poisson, §§V–VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0502028v1)

The reduction, source algebra, graph constraint, scalar normalization, and two-polarization equivalence follow analytically in the ordered vacuum hierarchy. The bounded manufactured tests are floating-point checks. They do not establish the physical pole enclosure, analytic continuation, or any finite-amplitude remainder. Independent higher-order canonical data change the residue. For example add a compact returning RW displacement or velocity with late earliest cut. Product Proca reconstruction gives compatible canonical data and finite propagation leaves all earlier cuts unchanged, while the late data generally have a nonzero pole projection. This tests the dependence on the pinned preparation. The comma separators in the displayed slice line element in FINITE-DATA are typographical, with its inverse and subsequent equations corresponding to $-f\,d\tau^2+2s_r\,d\tau\,dr+(1+s_r)dr^2+dz^2$.
