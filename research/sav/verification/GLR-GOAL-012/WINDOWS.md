## The two subtractions and the all-late-bin theorem

The difference contains the prompt, continuum, and all other contributions and can cancel the shifted pair. In particular \(\|N_{21}-Q\|_2\) has no lower bound from \(\|S_*\|_2\) alone.

## Clock, cuts and finite seeds

Use $u_{\rm old}=u_{\rm new}+\epsilon^2c$. The returned shear changes by $cN_{01}$ and its news by $cN_{01}'$ at fixed coordinate labels. Thus $P=\langle N_{01},N_{21}\rangle_I$ changes by $c[N_{01}^2]_{\partial I}/2$, with the same overall prefactor in the physical energy. If the same physical interval is pulled back, the displacement of its endpoints cancels this term from the leading flux. A common absolute translation changes a shear residue by the exponential rescaling corresponding to its pole. A relative infinitesimal translation changes $A_{21,q}$ by $c s_q A_{01,q}$, leaving $\operatorname{Im}(A_{21,q}\overline{s_q A_{01,q}})/|s_q A_{01,q}|$ unchanged. The inherited interval for this quantity is strictly negative. The compact baseline transform is holomorphic at $s_*$ given the local returning inverse certificate, so an inertial constant translation cannot alter the shifted excitation. A formal noninertial time change $u_{\rm old}=u_{\rm new}+\epsilon^2 a e^{2\Omega u}$ would add $a e^{2\Omega u}N_{01}$ to the shear coefficient, creating an apparent shifted pole. The time-dependent lapse of that transformation is excluded by the inertial Bondi frame. A decaying local change of master $Z$ must change the entire graph jet and add $L_{\rm RW}Z$ to the source. The retarded solution changes by exactly $Z$, with zero null trace for a decaying extraction change. Angle-dependent BMS cut changes can change a mode projection and require pullback. For a varied geometric ADM mass $M$, fixed physical time gives $\partial_M s_q=-s_q/M$ and $\partial_M(Ae^{s_q u})=(\partial_M A+uA\partial_Ms_q)e^{s_q u}$. This generates both simple and double poles. The common-clock quotient is a fixed-mass statement.

For a finite-seed interpretation require a bounded nonlinear trace error in addition to small local fields. One possible theorem starts from a smooth constrained family at fixed charges and fixed Bondi frame, with half-period symmetry in $\epsilon$. Subtract its exact GL-free news at the same $\eta$ and project to the axial quadrupole. If the finite-window map has sufficiently many bounded mixed derivatives and no axial radiation at $\eta=0$, then a suitable quantitative target is
$$
\|N^{\mathrm{excess}}_{\epsilon,\eta}-\epsilon^2\eta N_{21}\|_{L^2(I)}
 \le C_I(|\epsilon|^4|\eta|+\epsilon^2\eta^2).
$$
The two terms follow from evenness and the mixed Taylor remainder under these assumptions. A tested dominance margin must exceed this error divided by $\epsilon^2|\eta|$. The full finite-seed signal also contains the ordinary $\eta N_{01}$. On a finite strip, a smallness norm must bound regular GL jets throughout the causal region, with exponential radial decay compensating the growth at large $v$, and satisfy $|\epsilon\chi_\infty|/r_0\ll\delta$ for the short-packet phase expansion. For fixed seed, indefinite growth at any fixed regular radius leaves that regime. Formal all-late-window conclusions refer to the ordered solution only.

## Ordered data counterexample at both fixed residues

Keep the lower fields and GL source unchanged. A compact mean upper homogeneous CPM solution $w$ is an admissible ordered change. On $\Sigma$, its two free canonical differences are $\pi^x=\lambda r w/2$ and $A_x+pA_t=(D_\Sigma w)/(2r)+pf w/(2r^2)$, where $D_\Sigma=\partial_t+p\partial_x$. They allow independent smooth real displacement and normal jet. The graph is spacelike and the data vanish on a horizon collar. The axial upper change leaves the spherical charges fixed at the order considered.

The induced Bondi news on any bounded strip can be changed independently of the ordinary and translated poles. Let the earlier edge be $a$ and prescribe outgoing graph data $w|_\Sigma=A(u)$, $w_v|_\Sigma=0$, with $A(u)=\int_a^u q$. Put $D(u,v)=V_{\rm RW}/4$ and $J(u)=\int_{v_\Sigma(u)}^\infty D\,dv\le M=9/4$ in units $r_0=1$. Integrating $w_{uv}=-Dw$ first in $v$ and then in $u$ gives $w=A-\mathcal T w$. Where a constant-$v$ segment first meets the graph, its left value is $A$ evaluated at that intersection. Equivalently the $\mathcal T$ integrand is extended by zero before that intersection. Thus
$$
\sup_v |\mathcal T w(u,v)|\le M\int_a^u \sup_v|w(z,v)|\,dz,
\qquad
\sup_v|w(u,v)|\le e^{M(d-a)}\int_a^u |q(z)|\,dz
$$
on $a\le u\le d$. The news $H=q+\mathcal Kq$ is the $v\to\infty$ limit of the $u$ derivative, and $|\mathcal Kq(u)|\le M e^{M(d-a)}\int_a^u |q(z)|dz$. The Volterra powers give factorial bounds, so $I+\mathcal K$ is invertible on continuous profiles. Smooth outgoing canonical data give a dense range in continuous news on the tested interval by approximating $q$ with smooth profiles vanishing at the left edge. A smooth target and smooth graph kernel allow differentiation of the Volterra equation. In either version the displacement can be made compact by a taper after $d$, which leaves all cuts through $d$ unchanged.

The residue of a second homogeneous packet supported where $u_\Sigma>d$ has rank two as a real map to $\mathbb C$ and its news vanishes on the tested interval. To prove rank, write $w_q=e^{-s_qt}h(s_q,x)$. The bilinear graph pairing tests $w_q|_\Sigma$ and $D_\Sigma w_q|_\Sigma$ independently. If all complex outputs lay on one real line, both weights would lie on a common constant complex line throughout the support interval. So would the tangential derivative of the first. Their difference after multiplying that derivative by $p$ is $(1-p^2)\partial_t w_q$. This is $-(1-p^2)s_qw_q$, inconsistent with common phase where $w_q\ne0$ because $\operatorname{Im}s_q\ne0$. Thus the late packet can restore the ordinary residue changed by the early packet. The two compact homogeneous additions are regular at $s_*$ since the returning RW inverse is regular there and their Cauchy numerators are entire. These ordered preparations therefore admit arbitrary finite-window remainder sizes and either signed interference with both pole residues unchanged. The additions are changes of independent upper radiation, excluded from the pinned preparation. Separately, in the fixed-canonical rule a different normalized lower profile can lie in the kernel of $A_{01,q}:C_c^\infty\to\mathbb C$. Three independent compact real profiles suffice by dimension counting, followed by derivative-square normalization. The nested pole transfer then gives zero shifted excitation for that profile, without asserting an ordinary returning residue for it.
