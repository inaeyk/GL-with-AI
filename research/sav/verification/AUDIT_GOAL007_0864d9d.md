## 1. Selection and Einstein hierarchy

Use signature \((-++++)\), \(r_0=2M\) for geometric \(M\), \(G_4=G_5/L\), and \(k=2\pi/L\). The real standing mode is \(e^{\Omega t}H(r)\cos(kz)\), completed in nonzero-mode unitary gauge and normalized by \(H_{\theta\theta}/(2r^2)=1\) at \(r=2r_0\). Each complex Fourier coefficient is half the cosine amplitude. The axial calculation is linear in the independent seed \(\eta\). Write \(H,P,B,J,K\) for the \((1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1),(2,1)\) coefficients, respectively, without factorials.

Translation in \(z\) and \(O(3)\) equivariance make Fourier indices add and angular products follow Clebsch–Gordan and parity. Because the GL tangent is \(0^+\) on the sphere, multiplying a quadrupole by it keeps its \(\ell,m\) and parity. \(HH\) is spherical in \(n=0,\pm2\). \(HP\) is quadrupolar in \(n=\pm1\), and \(HJ,BP,HHP\) can return a quadrupole to \(n=0\). In \(BP\), only \(B_0\) can do this. The two opposite-sign products of a real cosine give \(\langle\cos^2(kz)\rangle=1/2\); an odd compact factor times an even one has zero mean. A half-period translation implements \(\epsilon\mapsto-\epsilon\) for covariant preparations. Exact spherical symmetry at \(\eta=0\) forbids trace-free Bondi shear. Compact reflection removes the mean vector, and axial parity excludes a mean scalar linear in \(\eta\) at \((2,1)\). Polar parity permits it. For \(h=g-\bar g\), set \(\mathcal C(h)^a{}_{bc}=\bar g^{ad}(\bar\nabla_bh_{dc}+\bar\nabla_ch_{db}-\bar\nabla_dh_{bc})/2\). The exact connection difference is \(C=(1+h^\sharp)^{-1}\mathcal C(h)\). Using \(R(g)-R(\bar g)=DC+C*C\) gives diagonal second and third variations
