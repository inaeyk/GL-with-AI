Read [REPORT.md](REPORT.md) for the result and its scope,

A minimal nonspherical comparison would need two independent
small seed amplitudes on one finite horizon-regular slice:

- A physical compact standing GL tangent $n=\pm1$, $\ell=0$,
  normalized in the nonzero-mode unitary representative.
- One smooth ordinary $n=0$, $\ell=2$ axial tensor packet,
  with a specified canonical pair and comparison at $\epsilon=0$.
- The spherical quadratic constraint data and the driven
  $n=\pm1$, $\ell=2$ field including its compact component.
  The mean cubic Einstein source requires the mixed field
  and the mean spherical second-order field as well as the
  direct cubic term.
- A common fixed-mass Bondi frame, sphere parity and compact
  projection. Extract the $\ell=2$ massless shear and its
  retarded derivative separately from the scalar and massive
  channels. The leading cross energy uses the baseline and
  corrected tensor news in that same frame.

A future analytic ringdown calculation can prescribe a smooth
$\ell=2$ packet near the Schwarzschild potential barrier and
compute its finite-slice massive response and mean sourced CPM
waveform with [the full axial density equations](INVESTIGATOR.md).
Record the chosen canonical data and upper homogeneous data.
Compare the resulting waveform and QNM residues with the
ordinary finite packet, and compare at fixed ADM mass and in
the same Bondi clock. Numerical validation would require
controlled source tails, horizon regularity, causal truncation
error and a differentiated radiation trace. The early far-zone
front gives a separate small-amplitude sign and selection
benchmark. It does not set the scale of a near-barrier signal.

The rotating results are linear and literature-based. The
merger uplift formulation is exact, and the neck criterion
is conjectural. See [LITERATURE.md](LITERATURE.md) and
