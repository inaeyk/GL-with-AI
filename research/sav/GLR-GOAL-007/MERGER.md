# Exact merger uplift and a transient amplification criterion

Let $g_{\rm m}$ be a specified asymptotically flat four-dimensional
vacuum merger development, and let $z$ have fixed period $L$.
The product $G_{\rm m}=g_{\rm m}+dz^2$ is an exact five-dimensional
vacuum solution because the product Ricci tensor is the direct sum.
Its initial induced metric and extrinsic curvature are the four-
dimensional Cauchy data extended trivially in the circle direction.
Its tensor news and corresponding energy are the original merger
news and energy with $G_4=G_5/L$. The radion and KK vector are zero.
The exact $z$-isometry and uniqueness propagate this sector.
An arbitrarily nonlinear merger inside this sector has no classical
source for a nonzero Fourier coefficient from zero compact seed.

For a specified nonzero $n$ tangent, fix the unitary representative
using [the same compact gauge inversion](HIERARCHY.md), with
$\bar\nabla$ now that of $g_{\rm m}$. The $zz,z\mu$ equations give
$\operatorname{tr}_4 H_n=0$ and $\nabla^\mu H_{n,\mu\nu}=0$.
The other equations are
$$
(\Box_{g_{\rm m}}+2\operatorname{Riem}_{g_{\rm m}}-k_n^2)H_n=0,\qquad
k_n=2\pi n/L.
$$
Use finite regular compatible data on a fixed horizon-penetrating
merger slice, with controlled outer conditions and no independent
incoming boundary wave. Evolution is a time-dependent constrained
propagator. No stationarity or spherical separation is required
for the equation. At order $\epsilon^2$ about the exact merger
uplift, the KK-zero Einstein perturbation has source
$-\widehat Q(H_+,H_-)-\widehat Q(H_-,H_+)$, with the
connection map in [the hierarchy](HIERARCHY.md) evaluated on
$G_{\rm m}$. The same quadratic coefficient also sources a
radion. A retarded massless tensor correction must be extracted in
the merger's matched Bondi frame, with its own compatible data.
Expanding $g_{\rm m}$ about Schwarzschild with leading quadrupole
$\eta$ recovers the $(2,1)$ channel, including the change of the
first massive response.

For an operational amplification measurement choose a geometric
observer or foliation, a norm on finite constrained $H_n$ data
and a map onto a specified region of the evolving horizon.
A time-dependent propagator can mix existing unstable, damped and
continuum components. Comparing its gain with propagation on
appropriate initial and final reference strings distinguishes
excitation or increased gain of a pre-existing GL instability
from a proposed instability localized to the merger. A finite-time
gain in a particular norm also allows nonmodal transient growth.

A candidate local criterion applies **if** a patch is approximated
by a uniform Schwarzschild string with a slowly varying sphere
radius $r_{\rm loc}(\tau)$, usable proper time $\tau$, approximate
roundness and slow variation of the ambient geometry across the
support of its putative mode. The compact wave number is measured
with the local physical circle scale. In the exact uplift that
scale is constant. If there is an isolated growing branch with
enough spectral separation and small coupling to other modes,
instantaneous mode transport would give
$$
{\cal I}_n=\int_{\tau_{\rm in}}^{\tau_{\rm out}}
 \frac{\widehat\Omega_{\rm GL}(|k_n|r_{\rm loc}(\tau))}
      {r_{\rm loc}(\tau)}\,d\tau ,
\qquad
{\cal I}_n\gtrsim \log(A_{\rm target}/|A_{\rm seed}|).
$$
The integral is over the time in the candidate unstable regime,
and the comparison also needs losses before and after that window.
For a roughly constant rate the criterion is
$\Omega_{\rm loc}\Delta\tau\gtrsim1$ for one e-fold. A large
integral alone does not prove capture, support of the mode, or
growth through a changing neck. A joining horizon section is
slicing-dependent, its local shape need not approximate an
$S^2$ times the KK circle, and the available evolution time can
be of the order of the shape-change time or inverse spectral
gap. The criterion remains a conjectural estimate until a local
matching or a controlled propagator comparison supplies these
hypotheses. The reported noncompact higher-dimensional merger
analogues probe a different geometry and observable.
