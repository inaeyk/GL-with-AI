## Frame and measurable quantities

Use the circle normalization
\[
 ds_5^2=e^{-2\phi/\sqrt3}ds_{4,E}^2+e^{4\phi/\sqrt3}dz^2
\]
in the asymptotic scalar sector with \(A_\mu=0\), \(z\sim z+L\) and \(G_4=G_5/L\). In vacuum the order-two physical scalar is the completed compact zero mode \(\phi=\epsilon^2\phi_2\). The gauge completion follows from the \(n\ne0\) first-order unitary representative and the periodic average of the second-order \(zz\) equation. It removes contributions from coordinate changes in \(z\) that leave the circumference fixed. On geometric Einstein cuts the quadratic radiation coefficient satisfies \(\Phi_{\rm phys}=\epsilon^2r_0\Phi_Z(u/r_0)\) and \((dE/du)_{\rm phys}=(\partial_u\Phi_{\rm phys})^2/G_4\) at leading order. Thus \(E_I/M_0=\epsilon^4\mathcal K_Z\) at that order. The exact finite-amplitude use of this relation needs the vacuum trace and clock estimates. For a distant detector the coupling must be specified separately. In the idealization \(g_{\rm det}=e^{2a\phi}g_E\), the scalar contribution to the linear local tidal tensor is \(R_{0i0j}=a(\partial_i\partial_j\phi-\delta_{ij}\partial_t^2\phi)\). The five-dimensional external metric gives \(a=-1/\sqrt3\) for this coupling, with \(\delta g_{zz}=4\phi/\sqrt3\) along the internal direction. The flat far-zone monochromatic approximation at physical frequency \(\omega>m_\phi\) has transverse tide \(a\omega^2\phi\) and radial tide \(am_\phi^2\phi\), so their ratio is \(m_\phi^2/\omega^2\) for this conformal response. The massless radial tide vanishes to leading plane-wave order. Near-zone gradients, Schwarzschild curvature, apparatus motion and other matter couplings lie outside this detector formula. The signed flux coefficient itself is independent of \(a\).

At large radius a smooth band away from threshold has \(p_\omega=\sqrt{\omega^2-m_\phi^2}\) and radial phase \(p_\omega x_{\rm phys}+(m_\phi^2 r_0/2p_\omega)\log(R/r_0)\). Its group delay relative to a light ray has leading term \(x_{\rm phys}(\omega/p_\omega-1)\), plus a logarithmic and an emission phase correction. For \(m_\phi/\omega\ll1\), the leading term is \(x_{\rm phys}m_\phi^2/(2\omega^2)\). This scale explains why the actual Z joint limit depends on \(\Lambda=\mu^2x=m_\phi^2r_0x_{\rm phys}\). ## Completed sources and distinguishable spectra

One completion multiplies the Z forcing to the future of its graph by a smooth cutoff \(\chi(u)\) equal to one for \(u\le-8\) and zero for \(u\ge u_c>-8\). The forcing is smooth up to the initial graph on the future side. Its extension by zero includes a jump in \(F\) at the graph, with continuous zero field and first derivatives there. It preserves the full early source and all early traces by causal uniqueness. Only \(r\ge R(u_c)>1\) contributes. As \(t\to-\infty\) the active support requires \(r\ge1+e^{-t}\). As \(t\to+\infty\) it requires \(x\ge t-u_c\). The exponential GL falloff with \(2q-s>0\) then implies \(\int_{\mathbb R}\|F(t,\cdot)\|_{L^2_x}dt<\infty\). The nonnegative self-adjoint radial operator gives a finite-energy retarded solution with \(\sqrt{2E(t)}\le\int_{-\infty}^t\|F(\tau)\|_2d\tau\), with master energy in dimensionless units. This follows by the energy inequality or Duhamel with the unitary wave group.

On a compact band above threshold, let \(h_L,h_R\) have unit ingoing and outgoing asymptotic amplitudes, with the Coulomb phase included in \(h_R\), and set \(W=h_L h_R'-h_L'h_R\). The sourced outgoing amplitude is \(-W^{-1}\int h_L\widehat F\,dx\). The horizon amplitude uses \(h_R\) in its numerator. The analytic tail of the completed Z source is required for these overlaps. The band energies with physical units restored have the respective weights \(\omega p_\omega/(\pi G_4)\) and \(\omega^2/(\pi G_4)\). A real smooth source perturbation compactly supported in \(u>u_c\) can change the above-threshold band while preserving the early waveform exactly. Its coefficient changes quadratically with the amplitude of the added forcing and has a strictly positive leading quadratic term for an appropriate localized bump. The constructive proof uses the nonzero horizon Wronskian and a short time bump. The early GL growth exponent alone therefore fails to determine the timelike spectrum.
