## Conformal and hyperboloidal model check

A full five-dimensional conformal metric \(x^2(g_{\rm Schw}+dz^2)\) with \(x=r^{-1}\) has a degenerate circle component at the boundary even in the background. To see the issue at the equation level, put \(s=r_*\), \(u=t-s\), \(f=1-r_0/r\), \(A=fx^2\), and let \(q=r\varphi\) for a radial scalar of mass \(\mu>0\). With \(\left(-\partial_t^2+\partial_s^2-f(\mu^2+r_0/r^3)\right)q=S\) in \((t,s)\), the exact equation in \((u,x)\) is
\[
 2q_{ux}+\partial_x(Aq_x)-Bq=S/A,\qquad
 B=\mu^2 x^{-2}+r_0 x .
\]
The principal circle derivative is \(x^{-2}\partial_z^2\) before projecting. Multiplication by \(q_u\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \tfrac12\partial_u(A q_x^2+Bq^2)
 =\partial_x(q_u^2+Aq_xq_u)-q_u S/A .
\]
The compact contribution to the outgoing energy is positive, with \(\int\mu^2 x^{-2}q^2\,dx=\int\mu^2q^2\,dr\). A radial truncation has a boundary flux in this identity. It must be justified before taking \(x\downarrow0\). An uncommuted energy does not control the unsuppressed derivative \(q_u\) on an outgoing ray. On the static background the Killing commutator \(\partial_t=\partial_u\) gives the same positive compact term for \(q_u\).

For an elementary weight control, write \(v=t+s\) and \(4q_{uv}+Vq=0\), \(V=f(\mu^2+r_0/r^3)\). Multiplying by \(r^p q_v\) gives
\[
 \partial_u(2r^p q_v^2)+\partial_v(r^pVq^2/2)
 =2(\partial_u r^p)q_v^2+\tfrac12\partial_v(r^p V)q^2 .
\]
For \(p>0\) the second term on the right is positive at large \(r\). Thus the usual outgoing positive-power multiplier lacks the simultaneous favorable massive and derivative bulk signs. In the \(x\)-equation a naive conjugation \(q=x^p w\) introduces \(2p x^{-1} w_u\), which similarly gives a positive term on the right of the forward \(u\) energy identity.

There is a weaker model strategy. For the mean-free product scalar, \(P=-\partial_t^2+\partial_s^2+f\partial_z^2-V_0\), \(V_0=fr_0/r^3\), commute with \(Z=t\partial_s+s\partial_t\). The identity
\[
 [P,Z]=-t f_s\partial_z^2+t(V_0)_s
\]
has an \(O(r_0/(R+t))\) top compact coefficient on a fixed early exterior \(s\ge t+s_0\), with \(R=r(s_0)\). It lowers boost count, and one extra \(\partial_z\) puts the source in the ordinary energy at the same total commutation order and lower boost count. The bound follows from \(f_s=f r_0/r^2\) and \(r\gtrsim R+t\) for large fixed \(R\). Higher \(Z\)-derivatives of this coefficient obey the same rate, since its radial derivatives cost inverse powers of \(r\) and \(t\lesssim r\). In the linear homogeneous model this gives a triangular estimate for energy square roots, with at most \(j\) logarithms at boost count \(j\) from integrating \((R+t)^{-1}\) against the lower ranks. \(\partial_t\) commutes before the boost and creates only lower-rank background terms if applied after it. On a fixed cut \(Z=t\partial_s|_u-u\partial_u\), giving access to weighted outgoing derivatives. The tensor connection terms and differentiated quasilinear principal coefficients require their own triangular control. This model cannot replace those estimates.

The same model calculation works with data on the tilted slice. For \(t<0\) the condition \(\tau\ge0\) implies \(r\ge r_0(1+e^{-t/r_0})\), so the bound on each differentiated boost source coefficient is past integrable. Integrate the Killing identity from \(\Sigma\), truncated by an incoming null side \(v\le V_{\rm cut}\) and a constant-\(t\) top. The fluxes on the two null sides contain \((q_t\pm q_s)^2\), \(f|q_z|^2\) and \(V_0 q^2\), all with nonnegative weights. The initial flux is bounded by the usual commuted data energy because \(\Sigma\) has slope bounded away from null at large radius. Source work for a field with \(j\) boosts is bounded by the integral of its energy square root times the lower-rank energy square root times the coefficient bound. Taking the running supremum of the top energy, and using the same identity for the outgoing flux, gives a rank-\(j\) energy and outgoing flux square root bounded by \(C_j(1+\log(2+\max(t,0)))^j\), with \(C_j\) set by the finite commuted initial energies. The estimates are independent of \(V_{\rm cut}\).

This implies stronger pointwise massive decay for the **homogeneous product scalar model**. On a large fixed-cut dyadic shell \(s\asymp D\), the flux controls the \(L^2(ds;H_z^\ell)\) norms of \(q\) and \(Zq\) via the circle Poincaré inequality, and of \(q_u\) via time commutation. Since \(W:=Z+u\partial_u=t\partial_s|_u\), rescaled one-dimensional Sobolev gives an initial shell supremum of order \(D^{-1/2}\) times the logarithmic factors, including finitely many commuted fields. The compact inverse applied to the equation gives
\[
 q=f^{-1}\partial_z^{-2}\big[-t^{-2}(W^2-W)q+2t^{-1}Wq_u+V_0q\big].
\]
This costs at most two spare conormal derivatives per iteration and gains one factor \(D^{-1}\) per iteration on a padded fixed \(u\) interval. Derivatives of the displayed coefficients preserve the indicated decay orders under \(W,\partial_u,\partial_z\) on that interval. The circle inverse is bounded on all periodic mean-free Sobolev spaces at fixed \(L\). Thus any prescribed finite polynomial gain in the homogeneous model follows with sufficiently many finite commuted initial energies. For a forced model, \(\partial_z^{-2}(S/f)\) occurs on the right, and conormal decay can improve only as far as the forcing permits. The exact Einstein problem requires controlling that term together with the differentiated compact-dependent principal coefficients.

A possible foliation intermediate is \(U=u-h(s)\), with \(h\to0\) and \(h'<0\) integrable. Its inverse norm is
\[
 g^{-1}(dU,dU)=g^{uu}-2h'g^{us}+(h')^2g^{ss}.
\]
If \(g^{us}\le -c\), \(g^{ss}\) is bounded, and the **full** eikonal defect obeys \(|g^{uu}|\le C r^{-1-\eta}\) for \(\eta>0\), choose \(h'=-C_1r^{-1-\eta}\) with \(2cC_1>C\). The leaves are spacelike near infinity and asymptote to the comparison cuts. In the static model the Killing energy on \(t=s+U+h(s)\) has density
\[
 \tfrac12(q_t^2+q_s^2+f|q_z|^2+V_0q^2)+(1+h')q_tq_s.
\]
The compact energy stays unsuppressed as \(h'\to0\); the transverse derivative has a vanishing coefficient. This identifies which hyperboloidal energy remains viable and which commutation is still needed. With an \(O(r^{-1})\) defect of uncontrolled sign the same sufficient construction would require a nonintegrable \(h'\) and could shift the clock by \(\log r\).

## Priority and search limit

The most direct exterior adaptation is the Schwarzschild-relative wave-map formulation with a compact-derivative energy and a causal or asymptotically spacelike side, using a good full inverse-null coefficient estimate to justify that side. A complementary geometric route is the full optical terminal construction once differentiated compact moments are available. A direct substitution of the \(3+1\) compactified inverse, a standard outgoing positive-power multiplier, or a massive timelike decay law does not close the massive-ray gate.

A sufficiently early nonlinear wedge could use \(I_A=[g(A+1),g(A)]\) in the dimensionless pinned clock, \(g(R)=-R-2\log(R-1)\), \(A\ge100\). The GOAL-005 radiation derivation gives a positive analytic enclosure for \(n_Z\) there using its nonzero normalized far profile and its own \(2\int_{I_A} n_Z^2\,du\). The later numerical enclosure on \([-10,-8]\) belongs to that benchmark interval.

The bounded search covered the cited primary theorem texts and searches for positive-mass KK exterior existence, compactification, massive Schwarzschild null asymptotics and terminal optical functions. It found no result with the full \(4+1\) damped Z data, positive-mass wedge, differentiated compact-mode estimates and normalized seed-differentiated scalar radiation trace. This is a scope finding about the inspected results.
