# Primary literature and applicability

The sources below use their own symbols. In GOAL-008, $r_0=2M$ in geometrized Schwarzschild units, $m_\phi$ is the radion mass, and $\mu=m_\phi r_0$. In the original GL literature a parameter denoted by $\mu$ or $\omega$ can instead be a growth rate or a string wavenumber.

## Massive monopole on Schwarzschild

- Hiroko Koyama and Akira Tomimatsu, *Asymptotic tails of massive scalar fields in Schwarzschild background*, Phys. Rev. D **64**, 044014 (2001). The inspected v2 PDF gives the radial potential and retarded Green function in §II, including the Wronskian and branch-cut integral in Eqs. (15)–(19). Equation (1) and §III.B, Eq. (33), describe an intermediate oscillatory $t^{-\ell-3/2}$ regime for small $Mm_\phi$. Section III.C, Eq. (36), places the late $t^{-5/6}$ regime at $m_\phi t\gg (Mm_\phi)^{-2}$, a time scale diverging like $m_\phi^{-3}M^{-2}$. These are tail regimes of the homogeneous evolution or Green function at a fixed observation radius. Application to the actual forced GL history requires control of the source continuation and its overlap with the threshold response. [Inspected arXiv v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0103086v2), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.64.044014).
- Federico Pasqualotto, Yakov Shlapentokh-Rothman and Maxime Van de Moortel, *The asymptotics of massive fields on stationary spherically symmetric black holes for all angular momenta*. Theorem I and Eq. (1.2) of the inspected v2 establish a $t^{-5/6}$ leading term with explicit oscillatory profile and $O(t^{-1+\delta})$ remainder, for a fixed nonzero field mass and smooth compact initial data with finitely many angular modes. The $s$-wave is included. The leading coefficient is a functional of the data and can vanish. Remarks 1.1–1.4 delimit the compact radial regime, the use of a horizon-regular time, and the error's mode dependence. In particular the result does not supply a uniform asymptotic in $(r,t,m_\phi)$. The threshold profile has $|u_H^*(r)|\lesssim r^{1/4}$ for the reduced field $u=r\phi$. [Inspected arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17767v2).
- Derek Lee, *Scattering of massive scalars by Schwarzschild black holes*. Section 2, Eqs. (8)–(9), distinguishes the Schwarzschild-coordinate phase $kr+[2Mk+Mm_\phi^2/k]\ln(r/r_0)$ from the tortoise-coordinate form $kr_*+(Mm_\phi^2/k)\ln(r/r_0)+o(1)$ up to a constant. Here $k=\sqrt{\omega^2-m_\phi^2}>0$ for positive real above-threshold $\omega$. The additional logarithm survives removing the tortoise logarithm and makes plane-wave extraction at infinity nonuniform near threshold. At equality the radial asymptotic must be handled separately. The leading equation $u''+(2Mm_\phi^2/r)u=0$ at large $r$ gives $r^{1/4}\exp[\pm 2im_\phi\sqrt{2Mr}]$ up to constant factors and subleading terms. This last statement follows by leading WKB balance and concerns threshold modes, whose single frequency has no weight in an ordinary pulse continuum integral. [Inspected arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1106.2709v1).
- Sam R. Dolan, *Instability of the massive Klein-Gordon field on the Kerr spacetime*, Phys. Rev. D **76**, 084001 (2007). Section II.B and Eq. (21) use the horizon flux to determine the sign of the imaginary part for the bound problem. Section II.D imposes ingoing horizon and decaying infinity data and distinguishes these states from radiative quasinormal boundary conditions. Section IV.B and Fig. 3 report the damped Schwarzschild spectrum, including $\ell=0$. Equation (31) and Appendix A, especially Eq. (A9), give the leading nonrelativistic hydrogenic real-frequency behavior. The rotating superradiance in the title has no Schwarzschild counterpart here. A pole in the frequency-domain problem leaves its GL excitation amplitude and eventual escaping energy undetermined. [Inspected published full text](https://harvest.aps.org/v2/journals/articles/10.1103/PhysRevD.76.084001/fulltext), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.76.084001).

## Circle stabilization and supporting stress

- Eduardo Pontón and Erich Poppitz, *Casimir Energy and Radius Stabilization in Five and Six Dimensional Orbifolds*, JHEP **06**, 019 (2001). The inspected v3 text distinguishes mechanisms in §2.2. The massive plus massless Casimir and bulk cosmological term mechanism also applies to the smooth circle. Brane-tension and brane-kinetic counterterms in the same paper require orbifold fixed points. Section 2.4, Eq. (18), gives the finite winding part of the massive determinant with the bulk cosmological term subtracted. The paragraph following Eq. (18) gives the $-4$ fermion-to-real-scalar factor on $S^1$ and the factor of $1/2$ in the orbifold calculation. Section 2.4, Eq. (19) and the following discussion give a three-term example with a tuned positive bulk cosmological term and a stable zero-energy minimum. Writing $x=m_f L$ for a fermion of mass $m_f$ and a circle of physical circumference $L$, the dimensionless massive factor is $\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-5}e^{-nx}[1+nx+(nx)^2/3]$. The Einstein-frame potential scales as $x^{-6}$ times the sum of this term, a massless constant, and a bulk term proportional to $x^5$. This is an asymptotic-vacuum model with a calculable supporting stress. Its flat-product determinant leaves the state-dependent stress tensor and its response in the black-string region to be computed. [Inspected arXiv v3 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/0105021v3), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1088/1126-6708/2001/06/019).
- Nima Arkani-Hamed, Sergei Dubovsky, Alberto Nicolis and Giovanni Villadoro, *Quantum Horizons of the Standard Model Landscape*, JHEP **06**, 078 (2007). Appendix A, Eqs. (35)–(36), computes the translation-invariant flat-product stress by the image Green function in general dimension and supplies the fermion sign. With an orthonormal circle direction and signature $(-++++)$, the homogeneous stress has noncompact part $-\rho g_{\mu\nu}$ and circle pressure $p_y=-\rho-L\partial_L\rho$. Thus the full homogeneous stress vanishes if $\rho_{\rm tot}=\partial_L\rho_{\rm tot}=0$. This derives from thermodynamic variation of the proper circumference as well as the image stress. The curved-geometry approximation in §3.1 before Eqs. (10)–(12) assumes the circle changes slowly and neglects derivative dependence of the quantum effective action. Its examples involve lower-dimensional AdS vacua and quantum extremal objects. They do not fix the response around a four-dimensional Schwarzschild factor or the GL spectrum. [Inspected arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0703067v1), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1088/1126-6708/2007/06/078).
- Yuichi Fukazawa, Takeo Inami and Yoji Koyama, *Radion Inflation in Higher-Dimensional Gravity Theory*, PTEP **2013**, 021B01. The $M_4\times S^1$ action and one-loop competition of gravitons and massive fermions are visible in Eqs. (1)–(9) of the inspected v1. Their choice in Eq. (16) fits both a five-dimensional bulk counterterm $a$ and an additive reduced constant $b$. That tuning does not independently establish an unwarped smooth-circle solution using only one higher-dimensional cosmological term. The smooth-circle criterion used here is the full-stress cancellation above. [Inspected arXiv v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.7305), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptep/pts088).
- Mark A. Rubin and Bernard D. Roth, *Fermions and stability in five-dimensional Kaluza-Klein theory*, Phys. Lett. B **127**, 55–60 (1983). The inspected publisher abstract gives an early explicit attribution for one-loop stabilization by massive fermions or twisted massive bosons with an adjusted bulk cosmological term. Full-text equation-level claims in this source were not inspected. [Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(83)91629-5).

For a Ricci-flat unwarped product, the 5D Einstein equation sets the total homogeneous stress to zero. The circle-directed null contraction $\rho+p_y=-L\partial_L\rho$ then vanishes. Conventional positive-kinetic classical winding contributes strictly positively to this contraction, while a bulk cosmological term contributes zero. The cancellation constraint thus rejects the simple winding-plus-cosmological-term proposal. The Casimir example is outside that classical matter class. This algebra uses the product equations and does not make a no-go claim for other matter systems or warped solutions.

## KK interactions and GL input

- James Bonifacio and Kurt Hinterbichler, *Unitarization from Geometry*, JHEP **12**, 165 (2019). For a circle, §2.6.1, Eqs. (2.88)–(2.92), gives the real sine/cosine mode overlap selection, equivalent to signed KK-number conservation in a complex basis. The massless graviton, graviphoton, volume modulus and massive spin-2 tower follow from §3.2.1, Eqs. (3.25)–(3.29). Section 3.2.2, Eqs. (3.30)–(3.34), gives the cubic gravity vertices relevant to massive spin-2 scattering. In particular Eq. (3.34) contains the radion coupling to opposite KK labels. Its vertex is an Einstein–Hilbert contribution on a Ricci-flat product and does not include response operators from stabilization. In a translationally invariant theory the allowed $\phi_0 h_n h_{-n}$ vertex permits a $+n,-n$ pair, or mixing in the presence of a seeded $n$ mode. It cannot source a single nonzero-$n$ field from purely zero-mode classical data. A quantum decay through that vertex has the additional flat-asymptotic two-body condition $m_\phi\ge 2m_{\rm KK,|n|}$. [Inspected arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.04767v3), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP12%282019%29165).
- Sam C. Collingbourne, *The Gregory–Laflamme Instability of the Schwarzschild Black String Exterior*. Theorem 1.1 and Eqs. (8)–(11) of v1 establish a smooth future-horizon, infinity-decaying vacuum growing mode on an explicit nonzero-wavenumber interval. The compact-circle remark below the theorem discretizes the wavenumber. The perturbation and its $\Omega$ refer to vacuum linearized gravity. This existence theorem does not provide a threshold or eigenvalue shift under a stress-tensor deformation. [Inspected arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.08441v1), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0043059).
- Richard Brito, Vitor Cardoso and Paolo Pani, *Massive spin-2 fields on black hole spacetimes*, Phys. Rev. D **88**, 023514 (2013). Sections II.3 and IV, particularly Eqs. (26)–(31), explain why a nonzero KK metric mode can be spherically symmetric and unstable as a massive spin-2 monopole in the vacuum string correspondence. Here the tensor mass is the KK momentum. It is independent of the radion mass introduced in GOAL-008. The published bimetric/linearized correspondence does not transfer to nonlinear stabilization dynamics. [Inspected arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.6725v3), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.88.023514).

## Scope and execution record

The targeted search found a constructive asymptotic vacuum example, an applicable Schwarzschild tail theorem under its data hypotheses, and vacuum KK interaction checks. The inspected sources did not supply a horizon-regular semiclassical stress response for a stabilized Schwarzschild string, a corrected GL eigenvalue, or the nonlinear source history. The smooth-circle determinant is therefore input to Level II vacuum matching and to the low-frequency exterior EFT. A near-horizon derivative expansion is not automatically valid. For the vacuum fundamental GL instability, $k_1r_0$ is below a threshold of order unity and $k_1=2\pi/L$, while the Casimir minimum typically has $m_fL$ of order unity. Hence the background curvature and time scales near the string need an independent validity check before integrating the stabilizing fields out there.

The literature specialist executed five bounded passes covering massive late-time evolution and resonances, flat compactification and stress, KK cubic and GL inputs, applicability and priority checks, and this claim map. No numerical experiment or repository mutation beyond this file was part of this work.
